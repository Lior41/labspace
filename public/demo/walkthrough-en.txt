Lab Space is a virtual science lab for families. This is the ages-four-to-six experience: simple controls, a prediction, and a shared discovery. No account is needed.

Starting without a prediction shows a helpful reminder. I choose Earth. A prediction is not graded: it gives us something to compare with the observation.

Both balls launch with the same speed and angle. Only gravity changes. The Moon’s weaker pull gives a longer journey. The animation follows deterministic calculations, with air resistance left out.

I save our observation in the notebook. It keeps the prediction, settings and result in this browser. It is a local family record, not a cross-device account.

Reopening the settings and switching to ages ten to fourteen reveals numerical speed and angle controls. Older children can change one variable and compare another run.

The grown-up planner offers a gravity activity with steps and a question to discuss. The visible badge says Prewritten activity. No AI provider is enabled here, and AI never invents the physics.

Requesting a dinosaur DNA laboratory is refused because that module does not exist. The planner stays inside the available experiments: predict, try, observe, and explore again.
